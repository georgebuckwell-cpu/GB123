02 The problem

Buying factory automation is slow, opaque and relationship-locked.

Food & beverage manufacturers spend six and seven figures on packing, palletising, filling and inspection lines — yet sourcing still runs on trade shows, cold referrals and whoever the plant manager already knows. Suppliers, meanwhile, burn budget chasing leads that never fit.

For manufacturers (buyers)

No single place to compare suppliers by use case, budget and lead time. Discovery takes weeks, decisions are made on incomplete information, and the “safe” incumbent often wins by default.

For suppliers (sellers)

Marketing spend is scattered across expensive exhibitions and broad directories. Leads arrive unqualified — wrong sector, wrong budget, wrong timeframe — and sales teams waste cycles on poor fit.

  • Fragmentation. Hundreds of specialist OEMs and integrators, no neutral aggregator that a buyer trusts.
  • Opacity. Pricing, capability and delivery speed are hidden until you're deep in a sales conversation.
  • High stakes. A wrong line specification is a capital mistake — buyers are risk-averse and crave a shortlist they can defend.

03 The solution

Describe the line. Get the right three suppliers.

Line Guide turns a fragmented search into a two-minute brief. Buyers set their use case, budget and timeframe; a fit algorithm ranks suppliers by use-case match, verified ratings, budget alignment and delivery speed. Suppliers maintain rich profiles and pay for visibility and lead flow. Buyers use it free.

1 · Set a brief

Use case, budget band, timeframe and volume. No account required, no sales gate.

2 · Get matched

The fit algorithm returns a ranked top-three shortlist the buyer can trust and defend internally.

3 · Connect

The buyer contacts suppliers directly; suppliers receive a warm, qualified, use-case-matched lead.

Packing & primary packaging

Case packing, cartoning, flow-wrap and primary pack automation.

Palletising & end-of-line

Robotic palletising, stretch-wrap and full end-of-line handling.

Filling & dosing

Liquid, powder and viscous filling, weighing and dosing systems.

Vision & inspection

Manual process automation

Replacing repetitive manual tasks with cobots and fixed automation.

+ Expandable

Why it wins

The matching taxonomy is the moat. Every buyer brief and supplier profile makes the fit algorithm smarter, the shortlist more trusted, and the next supplier's reason to pay for a Premium slot stronger. This is a classic two-sided flywheel — VelaSync's job is to spin it.

07 Go-to-market strategy

Solve the cold-start. Supply first, then ignite demand.

Every marketplace faces a chicken-and-egg cold-start. The winning sequence here is supply-led: seed enough credible suppliers that a buyer's first search returns a genuinely useful shortlist, then pour high-intent buyer demand onto that supply and use the resulting lead flow to convert suppliers to paid.

Phase 0
Weeks 0–4

Foundations & positioning

Get the machine ready to fire before a pound is spent on acquisition.

  • Category & messaging: define the category (“supplier discovery for food & beverage automation”) and write separate value propositions for buyers and suppliers.
  • Conversion funnels: a dedicated supplier sign-up page, plus one buyer landing page per hero use case — palletising, end-of-line, filling & dosing, vision & inspection.
  • Tracking: GA4 + event tracking on the two events that matter — brief submitted and supplier sign-up — feeding a live KPI dashboard from day one.
  • Prospect data: build the target supplier list from PPMA membership, Foodex / PPMA Show exhibitor lists, Companies House SIC codes and LinkedIn Sales Navigator.
  • Sales assets: supplier one-pager, cold-email sequences, LinkedIn scripts and a 90-second demo video.

The outreach engine

Two outbound motions run in parallel — recruiting founding suppliers, and pulling high-intent buyers onto that supply. Both are systematic, sequenced and measured, not ad-hoc.

Supply-side — recruiting suppliers

Who: Sales Directors, Business-Development Managers and MDs at OEMs, integrators and distributors.

Source list: PPMA membership, Foodex / PPMA Show exhibitor lists, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Companies House SIC codes.

Channels: LinkedIn connect + message, verified cold email, phone follow-up, warm intros via trade bodies.

Angle: the Founding Supplier offer — free months plus qualified leads from buyers actively searching. Lead with lead flow, never features.

Demand-side — reaching buyers

Who: Operations Directors, Engineering & Continuous-Improvement Managers, Plant Managers at F&B manufacturers.

Source: mostly inbound via SEO & ads, supplemented by targeted LinkedIn ABM and email.

Angle: describe your line requirement, get three matched suppliers in minutes — free. Lead with the buyer's pain.

Triggers: manufacturers expanding, investing or hiring — timed, event-driven outreach.

Reverse vesting returns unvested shares if VelaSync exits early, with good-leaver / bad-leaver terms — fair to both sides.

Exit & liquidity — how either side can step away

A good partnership plans its own ending. Three clean routes mean neither party is ever trapped:

End the engagement

Either side ends the consultancy on 3 months' notice. VelaSync keeps trailing commission on customers already won for an agreed tail (e.g. 12 months) — or that stream is bought out.

Commission buy-out

On a change of control or by agreement, Line Guide can buy out the recurring commission for a lump sum — e.g. 3–4× annual run-rate. Predictable liquidity for VelaSync, a clean cap for Line Guide.

Equity liquidity

Vested equity is realised via a company sale (with tag-along rights), a secondary sale to incoming investors, or a buy-back at fair value. Good-leaver terms preserve vested value.

Change-of-control & valuation

If Line Guide is acquired, VelaSync's commission and vested equity crystallise — the commission converts to its buy-out multiple and the equity participates in the sale. A pre-agreed valuation mechanism (an independent valuation or a set formula) governs any buy-back, so a stand-off over price is impossible.

Protecting both sides

IP assignment — everything VelaSync builds belongs to Line Guide. Exclusivity / non-compete — VelaSync won't build or grow a competitor. Commission clawback — on any revenue later reversed or refunded. Good-leaver / bad-leaver — vested equity is treated fairly; unvested equity returns. Confidentiality — mutual, and survives the end of the engagement.

01

Deeper monetisation of the core

~£180k/yr

Extract more from suppliers already on the platform — no new acquisition required. The fastest, lowest-risk cash because it's pure product configuration on top of what exists.

How it works

Auction premium visibility — featured slots and category sponsorship above the Premium tier; sell qualified-lead add-ons when a supplier exceeds its plan; offer a paid “verified & audited” trust badge.

Revenue model

£150/mo featured slot · £40 per extra qualified lead · £75/mo trust badge. All recurring or usage-based, high margin.

Illustrative economics

30 featured (£54k) + lead add-ons (~£80k) + 30 badges (£27k) ≈ £160–180k/yr on top of subscription.

Build effort & timing

Low — configuration, not new product. Cash within 0–6 months of switch-on.

Key risk & mitigation

Over-selling placement can erode buyer trust in the shortlist. Keep matching merit-based — featured slots add visibility, never override fit ranking.

02

Transaction & finance capture

~£600k/yr · biggest lever

Earn on the deal, not just the introduction. Machinery deals are large and almost always financed, so a small percentage of transaction value dwarfs subscription revenue — this is the single most valuable stream.

How it works

A success / finder's fee on closed equipment deals (self-reported, verified with the supplier); introduce buyers to asset-finance & leasing partners for a referral commission; attach insurance, warranty & service referrals.

Revenue model

1% success fee on closed deal value · ~2% introducer commission on financed amount · flat referral fees on insurance/service.

Illustrative economics

225 deals × £120k × 1% = £270k success fees; ~50% financed × 2% = £270k finance commission; +~£50k ancillary ≈ £590k/yr.

Build effort & timing

Medium — needs deal tracking, a finance partner and attribution. Cash within 6–15 months.

Key risk & mitigation

Deal leakage — buyers and suppliers transacting off-platform to avoid the fee. Mitigate by making the finance and insurance value-adds worth staying for, keeping the success fee modest, and rewarding verified-deal reporting rather than policing it.

03

Used & refurbished machinery marketplace

~£250k/yr

The truest “AutoTrader” parallel — a large secondary market with its own sellers, bargain-hunting buyers and faster decision cycles, served by the same platform and audience.

How it works

Dealers and manufacturers list used / refurbished equipment; monetise via listing subscriptions and success fees; cross-sell used options into the same buyer briefs when budget or lead-time favours it.

Revenue model

£200/mo dealer listing tier · 2% success fee on used sales · featured used listings.

Illustrative economics

40 dealers × £200/mo = £96k; 150 used deals × £40k × 2% = £120k; + featured ≈ £250k/yr.

Build effort & timing

Medium — a parallel listing flow with condition data. Cash within 9–18 months.

Key risk & mitigation

Trust in used-equipment quality. Add condition verification, inspection reports and seller ratings, and lean on the platform's existing verified-supplier credibility.

04

Data, insights & supplier SaaS

~£220k/yr

The platform's demand data and workflow are products in their own right — high-margin, recurring, and defensible because only Line Guide sees this flow of briefs.

How it works

Sell anonymised market-insight & pricing-benchmark reports; a lead-intelligence subscription (demand signals by category/region); SaaS tools for suppliers — quote, RFQ and pipeline management.

Revenue model

£1,500/yr insight report · £200/mo lead-intelligence · £100/mo supplier SaaS seat.

Illustrative economics

60 reports (£90k) + 30 intel subs (£72k) + 50 SaaS seats (£60k) ≈ £220k/yr, high gross margin.

Build effort & timing

Medium–high — SaaS tooling is real product work; reports are lighter. Cash within 12–24 months.

Key risk & mitigation

Data privacy and supplier sensitivity. Sell only aggregated, anonymised insight — never one supplier's performance to another — and be transparent about what's shared.

05

Market expansion — verticals & geography

3–5× the base

Not a single revenue line but a multiplier on everything above: point the same platform, matching engine and playbook at new buyer pools that use the same machinery.

How it works

Replicate into adjacent verticals — pharma, cosmetics, general manufacturing (shared machinery, new buyers) — then geographies: Ireland → EU (NL, DE, FR) → North America, seeding supply per market as at launch.

Revenue model

The same tiers plus every stream above, earned again in each new market.

Illustrative economics

Each comparable vertical or geography ≈ a fresh copy of the core. Two verticals + two geographies could push total revenue toward 3–5× the UK food & beverage base.

Build effort & timing

High — localisation and fresh supply-seeding per market. 18 months+, one market at a time.

Key risk & mitigation

Expanding before the core is proven spreads the team thin. Only replicate once the UK engine is a documented, repeatable playbook with predictable acquisition economics.

Sequencing the build

Ship in effort order: 01 Deeper monetisation first (weeks, pure config), then 02 Transaction & finance as the flagship revenue lever, with 03 Used marketplace and 04 Data & SaaS in parallel once the core is humming — and 05 Market expansion only after the UK model is a proven, documented engine. VelaSync would prioritise and build these as part of the growth mandate.

16 Risks & mitigations

The honest risks — and how we handle them.

▲ Cold-start / empty marketplace

Buyers won't return if early searches are thin.

Mitigation

Supply-led launch: seed founding suppliers per hero use case before driving buyer demand.

▲ Supplier willingness to pay

Suppliers may resist a subscription without proof of value.

Mitigation

Free founding period, then convert on demonstrated lead flow with clear ROI dashboards.

▲ Long B2B sales cycles

Capital equipment decisions take months, slowing proof of value.

Mitigation

Measure lead quality and buyer intent early; don't wait on closed deals to prove the funnel.

▲ Incumbent / directory competition

General directories or a supplier building their own tool.

Mitigation

Win on neutral, use-case-specific matching and buyer trust — a directory can't replicate the flywheel.

▲ Key-person / capacity

Growth depends on focused execution.

Mitigation

VelaSync brings a delivery team and proven playbooks; clear lanes keep product and growth from colliding.
