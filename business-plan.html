02 The problem

Buying factory automation is slow, opaque and relationship-locked.

Food & beverage manufacturers spend six and seven figures on packing, palletising, filling and inspection lines — yet sourcing still runs on trade shows, cold referrals and whoever the plant manager already knows. Suppliers, meanwhile, burn budget chasing leads that never fit.

For manufacturers (buyers)

No single place to compare suppliers by use case, budget and lead time. Discovery takes weeks, decisions are made on incomplete information, and the “safe” incumbent often wins by default.

For suppliers (sellers)

Marketing spend is scattered across expensive exhibitions and broad directories. Leads arrive unqualified — wrong sector, wrong budget, wrong timeframe — and sales teams waste cycles on poor fit.

  • Fragmentation. Hundreds of specialist OEMs and integrators, no neutral aggregator that a buyer trusts.
  • Opacity. Pricing, capability and delivery speed are hidden until you're deep in a sales conversation.
  • High stakes. A wrong line specification is a capital mistake — buyers are risk-averse and crave a shortlist they can defend.

03 The solution

Describe the line. Get the right three suppliers.

Line Guide turns a fragmented search into a two-minute brief. Buyers set their use case, budget and timeframe; a fit algorithm ranks suppliers by use-case match, verified ratings, budget alignment and delivery speed. Suppliers maintain rich profiles and pay for visibility and lead flow. Buyers use it free.

1 · Set a brief

Use case, budget band, timeframe and volume. No account required, no sales gate.

2 · Get matched

The fit algorithm returns a ranked top-three shortlist the buyer can trust and defend internally.

3 · Connect

The buyer contacts suppliers directly; suppliers receive a warm, qualified, use-case-matched lead.

Use-case coverage — the taxonomy that powers matching

Packing & primary packaging

Case packing, cartoning, flow-wrap and primary pack automation.

Palletising & end-of-line

Robotic palletising, stretch-wrap and full end-of-line handling.

Filling & dosing

Liquid, powder and viscous filling, weighing and dosing systems.

Vision & inspection

Quality, label and foreign-body inspection with machine vision.

Manual process automation

Replacing repetitive manual tasks with cobots and fixed automation.

+ Expandable

Conveying, coding & marking, and more as supply grows the taxonomy.

Why it wins

The matching taxonomy is the moat. Every buyer brief and supplier profile makes the fit algorithm smarter, the shortlist more trusted, and the next supplier's reason to pay for a Premium slot stronger. This is a classic two-sided flywheel — VelaSync's job is to spin it.

07 Go-to-market strategy

Solve the cold-start. Supply first, then ignite demand.

Every marketplace faces a chicken-and-egg cold-start. The winning sequence here is supply-led: seed enough credible suppliers that a buyer's first search returns a genuinely useful shortlist, then pour high-intent buyer demand onto that supply and use the resulting lead flow to convert suppliers to paid.

Phase 0
Weeks 0–4

Foundations & positioning

Sharpen the Line Guide brand story, build the supplier & buyer landing funnels, instrument analytics, and prepare outreach assets. Define the “hero” use cases to lead with (e.g. palletising, end-of-line).

Phase 1
Months 1–3

Seed the supply side

Hand-recruit a founding cohort of suppliers with a free or heavily discounted launch tier in exchange for a complete profile. Target credible OEMs and integrators across the core use cases so buyer searches never return empty.

  • Direct outbound to a curated supplier list (LinkedIn + email).
  • “Founding supplier” offer — free months in return for early commitment & testimonials.
  • Goal: a critical mass of profiles per hero use case before demand switches on.
Phase 2
Months 2–6

Ignite buyer demand

Capture manufacturers at the moment of intent with search-led content and targeted outreach. Every use-case page is an SEO asset and a paid-ad landing page.

  • SEO content hub: “How to choose a palletiser”, “End-of-line automation buyer's guide”, etc.
  • Google Ads on high-intent terms; LinkedIn to plant & operations managers.
  • Partnerships with trade bodies, industry media and consultants for referral demand.
Phase 3
Months 4–9

Convert & monetise

With demonstrable lead flow, migrate founding suppliers onto paid Basic / Plus / Premium tiers — selling on proof, not promise. Introduce Premium upsells and featured placements.

  • Lead-flow dashboards that make the ROI of a paid tier obvious.
  • Tier-up campaigns; case studies from early wins.
  • Referral loop — suppliers and buyers invite others.
Phase 4
Months 9–18

Scale & expand

Deepen category coverage, expand geographically (UK → EU), and layer in new revenue lines. Systematise the acquisition engine so growth compounds.

Who does what

VelaSync owns the growth machine end-to-end: positioning, funnels, SEO/content, paid acquisition, supplier outreach and conversion. Line Guide owns the product, supplier relationships at the account level, and the platform roadmap. Clear lanes, one shared scoreboard.

10 The VelaSync partnership

Paid on results first. Equity as it's earned.

VelaSync proposes a structure that keeps Line Guide's risk low and everyone's incentives locked together: we take a commission on the revenue we generate, and convert that track record into an equity stake once agreed milestones prove the growth model works. You don't give up shares for a promise — you grant them for delivery.

Phase 1 · Months 0–6

Commission on generated revenue

VelaSync runs the full go-to-market engine with little or no fixed fee. We earn a percentage of the new supplier subscription revenue we originate — so we only get paid when Line Guide gets paid.

  • 20–30% of net new subscription revenue VelaSync brings in
  • Paid for the first 12 months of each supplier's life (or a set term)
  • Minimal fixed cost to Line Guide — performance-aligned from day one
Phase 2 · Months 6–18

Convert track record into equity

On hitting agreed milestones, the relationship converts: VelaSync earns an equity stake that vests against continued performance, reducing or replacing cash commission.

  • Trigger on milestones — e.g. 100 paying suppliers or £45k MRR
  • 5–15% equity, vesting over time and tied to KPIs
  • Aligns VelaSync with long-term enterprise value, not just this quarter

Three ways to structure it — pick the risk/reward that fits

Why this is fair to both sides

Line Guide de-risks growth — you pay mostly out of revenue that wouldn't exist without VelaSync, and only part with equity once the engine is proven. VelaSync takes early risk on commission in exchange for a real stake in the upside it creates. Milestone-based vesting means the equity is earned, not gifted.

12 Risks & mitigations

The honest risks — and how we handle them.

▲ Cold-start / empty marketplace

Buyers won't return if early searches are thin.

Mitigation

Supply-led launch: seed founding suppliers per hero use case before driving buyer demand.

▲ Supplier willingness to pay

Suppliers may resist a subscription without proof of value.

Mitigation

Free founding period, then convert on demonstrated lead flow with clear ROI dashboards.

▲ Long B2B sales cycles

Capital equipment decisions take months, slowing proof of value.

Mitigation

Measure lead quality and buyer intent early; don't wait on closed deals to prove the funnel.

▲ Incumbent / directory competition

General directories or a supplier building their own tool.

Mitigation

Win on neutral, use-case-specific matching and buyer trust — a directory can't replicate the flywheel.

▲ Key-person / capacity

Growth depends on focused execution.

Mitigation

VelaSync brings a delivery team and proven playbooks; clear lanes keep product and growth from colliding.
