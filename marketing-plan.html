01 Executive summary

Win marketplace liquidity — not vanity traffic.

This plan optimises for one thing: getting enough qualified buyer briefs matched to paying suppliers that the flywheel becomes self-sustaining. Every move is tagged to an AARRR stage so it can be executed in priority order.

Three big bets, ranked by leverage
  • Solve the cold-start, supply-first. A marketplace with thin supply is worthless to buyers. Bet the first quarter on seeding credible founding suppliers per hero use case so a buyer's first search always returns a useful top-three. Demand generation only switches on once supply can absorb it.
  • Own the buyer's search moment with a use-case content engine. The compounding acquisition channel is SEO — buyer's guides and programmatic use-case pages that rank for high-intent, low-competition terms. Paid search is the accelerant that buys speed while the content matures, not the foundation.
  • Monetise on proof, then expand the take. Convert founding suppliers only once we can show them real lead flow (an ROI dashboard is the sales tool). Then layer the expansion revenue — transaction success fees and equipment-finance referrals — that dwarfs subscription.

02 Strategic frame

Positioning, ICP & voice — the one-page orientation.

What Line Guide is, in one sentence

The neutral, search-first marketplace that matches food & beverage manufacturers with the right automation suppliers — describe a line requirement, get a ranked top-three in minutes.

The category we're claiming

Category creation: “supplier discovery for food & beverage automation.” The mental model is AutoTrader for factory machinery — but the wedge against a directory is that Line Guide is a matching engine (an active, ranked shortlist) not a passive listing. Owning the neutral-matchmaker position is the moat.

Who we're for (two-sided ICP)

Supply side

UK OEMs, integrators & distributors in F&B automation. Buyer: Sales / BD Director. Stated problem: “we need more leads.” Real problem: wasted spend on unqualified, wrong-fit leads. What they buy: qualified, use-case-matched demand.

Demand side

F&B manufacturers investing in a line. Buyer: Operations / Engineering / CI Manager. Stated problem: “find a supplier.” Real problem: sourcing is slow, opaque and career-risky. What they buy: a defensible shortlist, fast — free.

Business-model logic

Buyers free (fills demand with zero friction); suppliers subscribe at £300 / £450 / £600. The compounding channel is the SEO content + data flywheel: more briefs → smarter matching → more supplier value → more paying suppliers → more budget for content. Revenue expands beyond subscription into transaction and finance take (see §8).

Brand voice — the non-negotiable

Do

Technical, precise, evidence-led. Lead with lead flow (suppliers) or buyer pain (manufacturers). Use: matched, verified, top three, use case, fit. Show trust signals — verified suppliers, ratings.

Don't

No hype or buzzwords. Avoid: revolutionary, disrupt, game-changing, seamless. Never oversell placement in a way that undermines the neutrality of the match.

Activation · aware → first value
05 Activation

The two “aha” moments

A two-sided marketplace has two activations, and both must fire for the flywheel to turn.

Supplier activation

Complete profile → first qualified lead. Remove all setup friction with concierge onboarding (VelaSync builds the profile), then get a real lead in front of them fast — the moment they believe.

Buyer activation

Submit brief → receive top-three match. No account gate, minimal fields, instant ranked shortlist. Every point of friction on the brief form is a lost activation.

  • Concierge supplier onboarding + a “your first lead” email. skills: onboarding, emails
  • Brief-form CRO — cut fields, test flow, instrument every step. skills: cro, ab-testing
  • “Your matches” buyer flow — fast, clear, credible shortlist with next-step nudges. skills: copywriting, signup
  • Tier prompts at value moments — surface Plus/Premium when a supplier sees lead value. skills: paywalls
90-day

Concierge-onboard the founding cohort · instrument the brief funnel end-to-end · ship supplier “first lead” and buyer “your matches” emails.

Retention · converted → stays
06 Retention

Keep suppliers paying, keep buyers coming back

Supplier retention is driven by proven lead flow; buyer retention is lower-frequency (capital purchases are periodic), so it's really about staying top-of-mind for the next project — and becoming a referrer.

  • Supplier ROI dashboard. Show each supplier the briefs and matches they've received — the single best retention and upsell tool. skills: revops · cross-refs §8
  • Lifecycle email flows. Onboarding, monthly lead recap, and win-back for lapsed suppliers. skills: emails, churn-prevention · idea #46
  • Buyer newsletter. Use-case insights that keep Line Guide top-of-mind between projects and feed referral. skills: emails, content-strategy
  • Annual billing default test — pull cash forward and cut churn. skills: pricing, ab-testing · cross-refs §8
90-day

Ship ROI dashboard v1 · supplier lifecycle emails live · define & instrument churn (currently unknown — see §13 open decisions).

13 Measurement, RACI & open decisions

How we'll know it's working

North-star metric

Qualified matches delivered per month — buyer briefs matched to at least one relevant, in-budget supplier. It's the truest measure of marketplace liquidity because it only moves when both sides are healthy. Financial guardrail: LTV : CAC ≥ 3 (currently ~19).

Leading indicators by stage
StageLeading indicators
AcquisitionOrganic sessions, brief-page CTR, cost per brief, outbound reply rate
ActivationBrief-form completion %, supplier profile-completion %, time-to-first-lead
RetentionSupplier monthly churn, leads-per-supplier, renewal rate
ReferralReferred sign-ups, referral share rate, partner-sourced briefs
RevenueFree→paid conversion, ARPU, tier mix, MRR growth
Review cadence

Weekly VelaSync ↔ founder ops sync · monthly KPI review against the shared dashboard · quarterly plan recalibration.

Open decisions — ranked by impact
  • Pricing billing period. Are tiers monthly or annual? Changes the entire revenue model. highest impact
  • CAC validation. Real cost per paying supplier is unknown until outbound runs — every projection depends on it.
  • Churn rate. Unknown; assumed ~85% annual retention. Instrument in the first 90 days.
  • Lead-hero use cases. Which 3–4 to concentrate on first (evidence: search volume × supply availability).
  • Ad budget ceiling. How much monthly paid spend Line Guide will commit once CPA is proven.
  • Attribution for transaction/finance revenue — needed before the expansion streams (§8) can be turned on.
